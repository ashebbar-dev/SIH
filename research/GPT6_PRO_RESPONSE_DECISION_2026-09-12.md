# Decision on the user-supplied GPT-6 Pro research response

12 September 2026. This is an assessment and work-priority decision, not a claim that either proposed experiment has been run.

## Overall assessment

The response is useful and substantially more actionable than a broad new system architecture. Its strongest choices are retaining the original two projects, requiring meaningful baselines, preserving negative results, and separating source-copy attribution from guilt/content authenticity. Its proposed 2–6-hour schedules are optimistic given missing raw-data adapters, a missing matched NHC filter and physical-test logistics. Neither novelty nor winning follows from its equations or acceptance targets.

## DHRUVA: accept D1 as a corrected feasibility lead

The turn-dynamics speed idea is the more distinct new research lead, but implementation needs corrections before numerical results would be credible. The fitted residual must account for nonzero nuisance-prior means. More importantly, a tight prior can produce apparent confidence without new turn information; use a separate data-only rank/observability diagnostic. Existing learned speed uses the same IMU, so a naive Kalman fusion cannot count the proposed update as an independent speed sensor. Preserve future-only updates and a common evaluation regime, with candidate acceptance and all-outage results shown separately.

The current hardcoded driver-A loader, privileged alignment and absent raw-data directory do not meet the proposed causal, untouched-group evaluation. Public M/Y1/Vta candidates exist; usable outage counts, reference quality, acquisition grouping and prior exposure still need verification. No matched NHC-EKF has been established in the inspected code. Therefore authorize a small analytic/provenance/excitation diagnostic first, not a claim of 20% improvement or a new production navigation engine.

Full evidence and corrected equations: [DHRUVA review](GPT6_PRO_DHRUVA_REVIEW_2026-09-12.md). The original Sol Ultra retains the existing matched speed-state pilot; do not duplicate that work.

## NISHAN: accept the staged receiver question, not 96 captures immediately

The existing code already has global refinement, channel-matched-reference diagnostics and conditional Chernoff threshold calculations. The incremental lead is local recipient-independent channel estimation plus a strong soft-decoding baseline. A likelihood expression alone does not specify that receiver; freeze its geometry/photometric/noise model and preparation/scoring separation first.

Start with surviving-signal measurements on a few matched fresh prints. If that gate is promising, develop the strong global soft baseline before the local variant and reserve new test pages. The quoted 96 images represent 24 underlying held-out printed artifacts with repeated captures, not 96 independent documents. Do not demand that collection before a workable calibration receiver exists.

The conditional tail-bound idea is reasonable under its declared independence assumptions, but the actual Bernoulli probabilities, numerical margin, data reuse and scope across receivers/captures must be audited. It does not solve operator framing, physical content authenticity or independent validator governance. Full assessment: [NISHAN review](GPT6_PRO_NISHAN_REVIEW_2026-09-12.md).

## Existing compact pilot is a separate experiment

The pilot already underway when this response arrived was completed without changing its predeclared settings. It compares a known shorter symmetric code with repeated versus larger carrier blocks. Its outputs are not evidence for N1's unchanged 52,500-position comparison. All original cases in the synthetic pilot already attributed correctly; lower JPEG bit error alone is not a higher attribution success rate. [Pilot decision and raw evidence](NISHAN_COMPACT_CARRIER_DECISION_2026-09-12.md).
